How clean is Britain's power?

The government wants clean power by 2030. How far has Britain got? Each chart shows the start of a line: draw the rest, then see how close you were.

Sources: DESNZ, Clean Power 2030 metrics; Energy Trends: UK renewables; Annual domestic energy bills; Final UK greenhouse gas emissions statistics; DUKES chapter 5: electricity.

Clean power means nuclear and renewables, excluding energy from waste and combined heat and power. Power station emissions cover the whole electricity supply sector. Electricity demand is electricity available in the UK: supplied plus net imports, including grid losses. Bills assume 3,400 kWh of electricity and 11,200 kWh of gas a year, and are in cash terms, not adjusted for inflation.

Forecasts come from NESO, Future Energy Scenarios 2025, the Holistic Transition pathway. Future demand is GB system demand by financial year. Future power emissions are from electricity generation and don't net off negative emissions from bioenergy with carbon capture.
